Our History

More than 155 years of brotherhood, service, and community in Pataskala, Ohio.

The Beginning

Chartered in 1868

Pataskala Lodge #404 received its charter from the Grand Lodge of Ohio on October 12, 1868 — just three years after the end of the Civil War. The founding brothers were farmers, merchants, and craftsmen who believed that a man's character, not his station, determined his worth.

The lodge was named for the community it has served since its first meeting: Pataskala, a Licking County village whose name traces to the Delaware people who long inhabited this land. From its earliest days, Lodge #404 was woven into the fabric of the town — its members helping to build schools, support families in need, and uphold civic life.

The original lodge hall, located on the main street of Pataskala, hosted its first Entered Apprentice degree in November of 1868. The lodge's first Worshipful Master led a group of just eleven founding brothers — a small seed that would grow into a lasting institution.

Through the Years

Lodge Timeline

Key moments in the history of Pataskala Lodge #404.

  1. 1868

    Charter Granted

    The Grand Lodge of Ohio grants a charter to Pataskala Lodge #404 on October 12. Eleven founding brothers meet for the first time under the lodge's warrant, electing their first Worshipful Master and officers.

  2. 1885

    First Lodge Hall

    The lodge acquires its first dedicated meeting hall in downtown Pataskala, providing a permanent home for stated communications, degree work, and fellowship. Membership grows steadily through the late 19th century.

  3. 1917–1918

    Service in World War I

    Many lodge brothers serve overseas during the Great War. The lodge holds special communications to honor its members in uniform and provides support to their families at home — a tradition of wartime brotherhood that would be repeated a generation later.

  4. 1941–1945

    Service in World War II

    Lodge membership surges in the years following World War II, mirroring a nationwide growth in Masonic membership. Brothers who served in the armed forces return home and bring new energy to the lodge's charitable and civic programs.

  5. 1952

    Peak Membership Era

    Pataskala Lodge #404 reaches its highest recorded membership during the postwar boom. The lodge is a hub of Pataskala social life, hosting dinners, community events, and degree nights drawing Brothers from across the 19th District.

  6. 1968

    Centennial Celebration

    The lodge celebrates 100 years of continuous operation with a formal centennial banquet. Grand Lodge officers attend, and a commemorative history of the lodge is published and distributed to members and their families.

  7. 2000s

    Renewed Focus on Charity

    The lodge formalizes its charitable giving programs, establishing regular contributions to Licking County food banks, scholarship funds for local students, and support for the Shriners Hospitals for Children. Community visibility grows.

  8. 2018

    Sesquicentennial — 150 Years

    Pataskala Lodge #404 marks 150 years of unbroken service to the community. A special sesquicentennial communication is held, and the lodge commits to renewed outreach and membership growth into its next century.

  9. Today

    Building on the Past

    Pataskala Lodge #404 continues its mission of Brotherly Love, Relief, and Truth. Active, engaged, and welcoming, the lodge looks forward to its next 155 years of service to the brothers and the broader Pataskala community.

Roots & Community

Part of Pataskala's Story

Pataskala Lodge #404 did not grow in isolation — it grew with the town. As Pataskala evolved from a small agricultural village to a thriving Licking County community, the lodge was there at each step: fundraising for local causes, providing fellowship to families in hard times, and quietly ensuring that the values of honesty and integrity remained central to public life.

Many of the men who shaped Pataskala's early history — its mayors, teachers, merchants, and farmers — were members of Lodge #404. Their work inside the lodge room reflected and reinforced their work in the community.

That connection between the lodge and the town remains strong today. When you join Pataskala Lodge #404, you join not just a fraternity, but a living piece of local history.
