The Craft Explained

What is Freemasonry?

One of the world's oldest and most misunderstood brotherhoods — explained plainly and honestly.

A Brotherhood of Good Men

Freemasonry is the world's oldest and largest fraternal organization, with roots stretching back to the stonemason guilds of medieval Europe. Today, it is a society of men — bound not by stone and mortar, but by shared values: integrity, brotherly love, and a sincere desire to make both themselves and the world around them better.

It is not a secret society. It is a society with some private traditions — much like any close-knit organization. Its members, lodges, and charitable works are entirely public. What remains private are certain ceremonial elements of its initiations, preserved as a tradition of fellowship and personal reflection.

Freemasonry is also not a religion. It welcomes men of all faiths who believe in a Supreme Being. It takes no position on theology and expressly forbids discussion of partisan politics or sectarian religion in lodge meetings.

The Journey

The Three Degrees of Masonry

Every Mason progresses through three foundational degrees, each a step deeper into the teachings of the Craft.

1°

Entered Apprentice

The Beginning

The first step into Freemasonry. The Entered Apprentice begins to learn the fundamental lessons of the Craft — the importance of self-improvement, moral conduct, and the value of community. This degree teaches humility and the necessity of education in all aspects of life.

2°

Fellow Craft

The Pursuit

The Fellow Craft degree emphasizes the importance of learning, industry, and skill. The lessons of this degree encourage the Mason to expand his knowledge, sharpen his mind, and apply his talents in service to others and to his community.

3°

Master Mason

The Completion

The highest degree of the Blue Lodge, and the foundation of all Masonic appendant bodies. The Master Mason degree conveys the most profound lessons of the Craft — mortality, integrity, and the immortality of the soul. A Master Mason is a full member of the lodge.

Honest Answers

Common Questions

No. Freemasonry is neither a religion nor a cult. It requires its members to believe in a Supreme Being — but that belief is personal and between the man and his God. Masonry does not define or dictate theology, does not offer sacraments, and does not promise salvation. Most Masons are active members of their own churches, synagogues, mosques, or other faith communities.

Lodge meetings — called "stated communications" — typically involve lodge business (minutes, finances, committee reports), fraternal discussion, and occasionally the conferral of degrees on new members. Meetings open and close with brief, non-denominational prayers and follow a structured, centuries-old format. After the formal meeting, members often share a meal and fellowship.

Freemasonry does have certain private forms of recognition — including grips and words — that have been used for centuries as tokens of brotherhood. These are not "secret" in any sinister sense; they exist as a tradition of fellowship and as a living connection to the Craft's stonemason heritage. Much of this information is freely available in published books and historical records.

Traditional "Blue Lodge" Masonry — the kind practiced at Pataskala Lodge #404 under the Grand Lodge of Ohio — is open to men. However, women may join affiliated organizations such as the Order of the Eastern Star, the Order of Amaranth, and others, which share Masonic values and often meet in the same buildings. There are also separate, independent Masonic bodies that accept women, though they are not recognized by traditional Grand Lodges.

In Ohio, you do not need to wait for an invitation. Any man of good moral character who meets the qualifications may petition a lodge directly. The tradition that "a man must ask to become a Mason" simply means that the desire must come from within — it cannot be pressured or recruited. If you are interested, reach out to us through our Contact page or visit a stated meeting.

Pataskala Lodge #404 holds stated meetings on the 1st and 3rd Tuesday of each month (September through June) at 7:00 PM, lasting roughly 90 minutes. Attendance is encouraged but flexible — Masonry is meant to enrich your life, not burden it. Members also have opportunities to participate in degree work, charity events, and district activities as their time allows.
